# Attractor-Guided Evolution (AGE)
## Agent-facing method corpus — v0.1

**Purpose:** Give an AI agent enough operational knowledge to apply AGE to an existing project and to generate the smallest useful set of skills, workflows, roles, and project documents. This corpus defines the method; it does not prescribe an agent framework or an ontology engine.

**Read first:** `AGENTS.md`, then `method/01-definition.md` through `method/09-boundaries.md`. For implementation, read `agent/BOOTSTRAP.md`, `catalog/SKILL-DESIGN.md`, and `catalog/WORKFLOWS.md`.

**Fundamental rule:** Do not optimize for proving an idea right. Optimize for warranted progress toward a governed attractor, including the option to abandon an attractive implementation.

### What AGE adds

- A durable but revisable attractor describing wanted capabilities, invariants, and limits.
- An evidence-driven route from consequential uncertainty to a small discriminating experiment.
- A decision rule that converts results into changes of the artifact, hypothesis portfolio, or explicitly governed attractor.
- Cross-domain immersion as a **candidate-generation technique**, never as proof.
- A research memory that captures rejected approaches and reasons for decisions.

### What AGE does not require

- Seven levels of derivatives; higher orders remain speculative.
- Treating coding, testing, discovery, or immersion as the same activity.
- Building a new engine, ontology, orchestration platform, or multi-agent swarm.
- Converting values or subjective experience into fake objective measurements.
- Running a hypothesis spike for every routine task.

### Operational architecture

1. **Attractor:** Where should the system become more capable, and what must not be compromised?
2. **Work routing:** Is this routine implementation, meaningful uncertainty, a directional conflict, or an idea-generation problem?
3. **Hypothesis spike:** What is the smallest fair attempt to distinguish a claim from a serious rival?
4. **Evidence verdict:** What was observed, under which conditions, and what cannot be concluded?
5. **Evolution decision:** What should be retained, altered, discarded, deferred, or escalated?
6. **Memory:** Which lessons must future agents inherit without re-litigating the same failed assumptions?

### Corpus map

- `AGENTS.md`: compact agent contract and decision priorities.
- `method/`: normative method semantics, gates, and boundary conditions.
- `catalog/`: instructions for decomposing AGE into skills and workflows.
- `contracts/`: prose-oriented minimum record structures; adapt to repo conventions.
- `examples/`: concrete demonstrations, including a research program with a working but uneconomic architecture.
- `agent/`: bootstrap assignment and acceptance test for a implementing agent.

### Normativity

- **MUST:** Required for claiming AGE conformance.
- **SHOULD:** Strong default; deviations need a stated reason.
- **MAY:** Optional capability depending on context.
- **PROVISIONAL:** Theory or design hypothesis, not an established rule.

### Source boundary

AGE is a proposed synthesis of the user's Hypothesis Spike practice, attractor-guided project evolution, and convergence/immersion practice. It is **not** represented here as an academically validated, established industry standard. Its value must be tested against simpler practices.
