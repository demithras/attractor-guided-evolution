# 08 — Progress signals and method evaluation

Measure progress against the attractor, not only throughput. Never maximize a metric that is disconnected from purpose.

## Suggested evidence families

- **Capability:** Can the artifact perform newly required operations?
- **Reliability and safety:** Does it preserve its invariants under relevant failures and misuse?
- **Comparative economy:** Is the solution justified against a strong conventional alternative when total cost is included?
- **Adaptability:** Can new evidence be incorporated without destabilizing unrelated functions?
- **Epistemic quality:** Can key decisions be traced from evidence to assumptions and revisions?
- **Learning efficiency:** Did the spike reduce decision-relevant uncertainty at an acceptable cost?
- **Human impact:** Are affected users, operators, and stakeholders better served by the change?

## Negative results as assets

A rejected claim can increase knowledge while producing no new code. Record which decision it ruled out and estimated work avoided, but do not invent monetary savings without evidence.

## Review the method against a baseline

AGE itself is a hypothesis. Compare with a simpler delivery or agentic-engineering approach on sufficiently similar tasks. Include planning overhead, review time, experiment implementation, rework, quality, and maintenance. If AGE costs more without improving meaningful results in a class of work, narrow its use there.

## Governance of proxies

Avoid a universal numeric 'distance to attractor' unless the domain warrants one. A compact scorecard plus hard constraints is often more credible than a single blended grade. When a human tradeoff is required, record who chose it and why.
