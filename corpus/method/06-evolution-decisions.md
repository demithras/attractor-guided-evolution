# 06 — Turning evidence into evolution

A verdict is not a product change. AGE requires an explicit **evolution decision** that respects the attractor, evidence scope, and available alternatives.

## Legal decision types

- **Retain:** Keep the implementation or current direction; explain what earned continued investment.
- **Revise:** Narrow the claim or improve an implementation; create a new protocol for material changes.
- **Replace:** Adopt a stronger rival, often a simpler conventional solution.
- **Pivot:** Change approach or problem framing while preserving approved core purpose.
- **Defer:** Collect additional evidence only if it is likely to influence a real decision.
- **Stop:** Retire the line of work when marginal research or implementation cost is unjustified.
- **Escalate:** Request human review when core priorities, authority, risk, ethics, or commitments are in dispute.

## Progress is not monotonic feature growth

Deleting code, discarding an ontology layer, changing the benchmark, pausing a road map, or shifting from custom architecture to conventional tooling can be forward progress if the decision is warranted and better serves capabilities and invariants.

## Decision gate

Before acting, document: what was learned, alternatives considered, why the chosen path dominates **within the recorded scope**, effect on the attractor, expected cost and risks, whether the change is reversible, what must be monitored, and who can approve it.

## Handoff to delivery

Approved experiments become ordinary engineering tasks with success criteria; they do not permanently require experimental rituals. If future evidence reopens the original assumption, resume the experimental loop. Separate experimentation from deploying changes with external effects.

## Learning memory

Retain major rejected claims and their strongest counterexamples in a searchable record. Do not repeatedly fund ideas already refuted in the same scope without new conditions. A prior rejection can be reopened when the environment, costs, or evidence changes materially; cite what changed.
