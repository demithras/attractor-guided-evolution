# 01 — Definition and scope

**Attractor-Guided Evolution (AGE)** is a practical method for guiding the development of an artifact or practice under uncertainty. It maintains an explicit direction, probes consequential assumptions, and lets reliable evidence change implementation choices and, through governed review, sometimes the direction itself.

AGE does not require the final architecture to be knowable in advance. It does require a meaningful answer to: "What ability or outcome are we moving toward, what are we unwilling to sacrifice, and how would we notice lack of progress?"

## The unit of progress

The unit of progress is a **warranted evolution decision**, not a line of code, green test, number of generated hypotheses, or completed ceremony. Decisions may retain existing structure, simplify it, change it, postpone action, or terminate a thesis.

## Primary distinctions

- **Artifact:** What actually exists or is practiced now.
- **Development:** Changes to the artifact.
- **Hypothesis-driven engineering:** Changes to development decisions through discriminating experiments.
- **Attractor guidance:** Changes to the criteria used to select and interpret those experiments, constrained by long-term intent.
- **Immersion / convergence:** External experience that may alter candidate generation, perception, or framing; an input channel rather than an automatically higher-ranked layer.

These are *operational distinctions*, not literal successive time derivatives. Formal 'order' language is a useful analogy only when each layer genuinely changes the decision mechanism below it. No seven-layer axiom exists.

## Suitable problems

AGE is most useful when requirements are uncertain, the correct architecture is contested, adverse outcomes are costly, external feedback is informative, or available solutions are surprisingly different in cost and capability. It also applies to skills, organizations, learning, creative practices, and research programs with appropriate evidence standards.

## Poor fits

Avoid full AGE overhead for reversible formatting, obvious bugs with known causes, routine implementation under settled requirements, or decisions where an experiment cannot plausibly improve action. Use direct delivery with basic checks.

## Completion condition

AGE is successful when decisions become better grounded and the artifact grows in relevant capability, robustness, adaptability, or economy, **relative to a fair alternative**. A project can conclude that its initial attractor is not feasible or worth pursuing. Stopping can be progress.
