# Attractor charter — content contract

The agent writes this record when a project starts or when a direction review is approved. Keep it concise, plain-language, and owned by an identifiable decision maker.

## Required sections

### Purpose
Explain the durable reason the system should exist. Do not name the presumed architecture as the purpose.

### Desired capabilities
Describe behaviors or qualities an observer could recognize. Include known user/operator context.

### Non-negotiable constraints
Explain what must remain true under pressure, including safety, authority, security, quality, ethics, and budget constraints as relevant. Cite the human source of each commitment.

### Tradeoff rules
Describe how to resolve ordinary tensions among speed, reliability, feature scope, simplicity, expense, and adaptability. If unresolved, record the open judgment.

### Exclusions
List things the system is not required to become and tempting implementations not yet earned by evidence.

### Progress and anti-progress signals
Give observable indicators of useful progress and failure; note which rely on human judgment.

### Uncertainties
Name critical assumptions about the capabilities or goals, without treating them as settled design facts.

### Decision rights and review triggers
Name who can approve normal changes and who must review fundamental changes. State circumstances requiring review.

### Status and change history
Mark as provisional, approved, or superseded. Include a versioned decision reference when possible.

## Review checklist

Would this charter still describe success if the originally expected technology were discarded? Are its non-negotiables operationally clear? Can an agent recognize when it is drifting away? Does the charter avoid claiming to settle values with empirical tests?
